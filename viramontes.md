## 4. Positions not supporting petitioners

### 4a. U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops (neither party, filed 2026-09-04)
[PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-238/423066/20260904120755267_25-238_25-566%20tsac%20UnitedStatesConferenceOfCatholicBishops.pdf). Counsel of record: William J. (surname cut off on cover; check before citing).

The only Supreme Court brief that leans toward the County. Takes no position on the ordinance's facial validity but asks the Court to rule "in a manner that preserves the traditional authority of political communities to regulate weapons capable of exceptional violence."

Argument:
1. Bruen and Rahimi require analogy to *principles*, not historical twins. The Church's method of distinguishing doctrinal development from rupture (Newman, Benedict XVI) mirrors that.
2. Three principles run through both American tradition and Catholic social teaching: a real but limited right of self-defense (Catechism §§ 2264-65); the state's duty to protect innocent life and the common good; and federalism, which the Church calls subsidiarity (Leo XIV, *Magnifica Humanitas* 2026).
3. AR-15s and mass shootings are the "unprecedented societal concerns" and "dramatic technological changes" that Bruen said call for a "more nuanced approach." Cites Lamont (2d Cir.) flintlock-vs-AR-15 comparison and Bianchi (4th Cir. en banc). Do not assume founding legislatures maximally exercised their power (Barrett concurrence in Rahimi; Dobbs; McIntyre).
4. Raw ownership numbers should not be dispositive. Common use is a corollary of the dangerous-and-unusual tradition, not a freestanding headcount. Cites the Washington Post figure that about 6 percent of Americans own AR-15s. Letting sales control would immunize any weapon that proliferates before it is regulated (quotes Easterbrook in Friedman on the circularity in both directions).
5. Historical tradition of locality-specific gun laws (1831 Ohio, 1847 Virginia, 1825 Tennessee town charters); McDonald's line that incorporation "limits (but by no means eliminates)" local experimentation.
6. Cites 1139 Second Lateran Council anathema on crossbows as precedent for distinguishing self-defense from weapons of extraordinary lethality. Cites RAND (Jan 2026) and Klarevas/Siegel studies on magazine and assault-weapon bans.

### 4b. Professor Akhil Reed Amar (neither party, filed 2026-09-02)
[PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-238/422739/20260902064845976_260826a%20AC%20Brief%20for%20efiling.pdf). Counsel: Christopher Duggan, Smith Duggan Cornell & Gollub, Lincoln MA.

Docketed "neither party" but **recommends reversal**, so pro-petitioner on the question presented. Adverse to petitioners on framework and on large-capacity magazines.

Argument:
1. The operative right against states is the Fourteenth Amendment's Privileges or Immunities Clause, not the Second Amendment. Heller "all but ignored" the Fourteenth; the relevant history is the 1860s (Bingham, Howard, Freedmen's Bureau Act of 1866, Harper's Weekly 1868 Waud illustration), not the 1790s.
2. Two-track test replacing Bruen's precursor-statute hunt: (a) strong protection for weapons a law-abiding, nonthreatening adult would use for self-protection; (b) for weapons used mainly for sport, only Glucksberg/Corfield-style protection against *outlier* laws, measured by counting states.
3. Criticizes Wolford v. Lopez (2026) line that any law within the plain text is "presumptively unconstitutional": the Second Amendment's text binds only the federal government (Barron).
4. AR-15s protected under both tracks: usable for self-defense, and roughly four-fifths of states (two-thirds of population) leave them alone.
5. **Urges the Court to reserve large-capacity magazines.** A 30-round magazine is not "ordinarily needed for ordinary self-protection." Thirteen states plus DC limit LCMs, all "blue"; striking those down nationally would repeat Roe's culture-war mistake.
Conclusion: "reverse the judgment below and gently tweak its framework."

### 4c. State of Illinois, Seventh Circuit amicus supporting defendants-appellees (filed 2024-09-04)
[PDF](https://assets.nationbuilder.com/firearmspolicycoalition/pages/6277/attachments/original/1725637729/2024.09.04_027_Illinois_Amicus.pdf)

Defended Bevis on both Bruen steps:
- Step one: assault weapons are not "arms" because they are close enough to "M-16 rifles and the like," which Heller said may be banned.
- Step two: the ban is "relevantly similar" to historical dangerous-and-unusual-weapon laws in justification (new forms of violence) and burden (minimal effect on self-defense).
- Distinctive contribution: the remand record from Barnett v. Raoul (Illinois's own ban) confirmed Bevis and refuted the claim that AR-15s differ materially from M16s.
Headings: I. The record developed on remand in Barnett confirms Bevis was correctly decided. II. Plaintiffs' arguments in support of reversal are unpersuasive.

### 4d. Cook County's own position (brief in opposition, 2025-10-29)
Preview of what respondent-side amici will echo:
- No circuit split: every circuit to decide the issue has upheld a ban (Lamont 2d Cir. 2025; Capen 1st Cir. 2025; Bianchi 4th Cir. en banc 2024; Bevis 7th Cir. 2023).
- Poor vehicle: Viramontes skipped discovery and forfeited factual arguments.
- Merits: AR-15s are "much more like machine guns and military-grade weaponry" than self-defense firearms; not materially different from the M16.
- Historical "moderate, proportionate self-defense" principle from Blackstone (4 Commentaries 182-83) and State v. Wells (N.J. 1790).
- Common use should be measured by open public *carry* (affray law; State v. Huntly, N.C. 1843), not ownership counts.
- Petitioners' reading would collapse both Bruen steps into "how many were sold?"

Classification script (Python; needs `pymupdf`, which is already installed on this machine):

```python
import re, os, html, json, urllib.request, pymupdf
UA={'User-Agent':'Mozilla/5.0'}
out=[]
for dk in ['25-238','25-566']:
    p=urllib.request.urlopen(urllib.request.Request(
        f'https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/{dk}.html',headers=UA)).read().decode('utf-8','ignore')
    for m in re.finditer(r'<td class="ProceedingDate">([^<]+)</td>\s*<td>(.*?)</td>',p,re.S):
        date,cell=m.group(1).strip(),m.group(2)
        txt=re.sub(r'\s+',' ',html.unescape(re.sub(r'<[^>]+>',' ',cell))).strip()
        entry=txt.split(' Main Document')[0].strip()
        if 'amic' not in entry.lower(): continue
        u=re.search(r'href=\s*(\S+\.pdf)\s',cell,re.I)   # hrefs are unquoted on this site
        if not u: continue
        u=u.group(1); fn='pdfs/'+dk+'_'+os.path.basename(u)[:90]; os.makedirs('pdfs',exist_ok=True)
        if not os.path.exists(fn):
            open(fn,'wb').write(urllib.request.urlopen(urllib.request.Request(u,headers=UA),timeout=90).read())
        d=pymupdf.open(fn); cover=re.sub(r'\s+',' ',' '.join(d[i].get_text() for i in range(min(2,len(d)))))
        s=re.search(r'((?:IN )?SUPPORT(?:ING)? (?:OF )?(?:THE )?(?:PETITIONERS?|RESPONDENTS?|NEITHER PARTY|AFFIRMANCE|REVERSAL)[^.]{0,40})',cover,re.I)
        out.append(dict(docket=dk,date=date,entry=entry,url=u,file=fn,support=s.group(1).strip() if s else 'NOT FOUND'))
json.dump(out,open('amici.json','w'),indent=1)
for o in out: print(o['docket'],'|',o['date'],'|',o['entry'][:95],'|',o['support'][:60])
```

Note: cover pages with letter-spaced text (e.g. "P E T I T I O N E R S") defeat the regex and print NOT FOUND; read those by hand.
